# Industry and stakeholder argument analysis: accuracy gates

This protocol applies to the October 20, 2026 FCC 26-41 E-Rate corpus snapshot. It is a research methodology, not a claim that the current machine classifications are verified.

## Target research questions

1. What policy outcome does each actual filer request on each FCC option?
2. What reasons does it give, and what mechanism is claimed to link a rule to a consequence?
3. How do identified edtech companies, industry associations, schools, school staff, consultants, consultant associations, telecom/network vendors and parents compare?
4. Which claims appear disputable or factually unsupported, including allegations of E-Rate misuse? **Not every allegation is true.**
5. Where do the positions conflict with NTIA's independently sourced *actual* agency position? A listening-session summary reporting others' views is never treated as NTIA endorsement.

## Separate identity, argument, endorsement and evidentiary truth

**Filing entity**: ECFS filing ID, verified source, filer, signatory, organization represented, client (if a representative), and confidence/review basis. A legal representative, a professional association and its members cannot be collapsed into a single filer or credited with one another's positions. Joint filings stay one filing in filing-level counts; a separate co-signatory relation table may show participants.

**Issue / requested action**: filing ID × specific FCC option × author-endorsed request. Position options: adopt; reject; amend/condition; alternative; discuss without endorsement; conflicting or ambiguous; not addressed after complete human review. The FCC notice's own policy proposal is not the filer's position. Quoted critics and citations from others are not endorsement.

**Argument / causal rationale**: separate repeatable reason entries associated with a particular filing/option/action. The same filing can present several reasons; never multiply it into multiple supporters.

**Evidence / factual claims**: verbatim source excerpt, exact source offset, document hash, source context, evidence form (data, study, legal text, personal experience, unsourced assertion), external reference and independent check status. Text appearing verbatim in the source establishes only transcription, not whether the claim is true.

## Priority codebook

- Funding: preserve E-Rate; sunset or reduce; target eligibility; revise discount matrix; keep special construction, MIBS or Category Two funding.
- Children and schools: federal screen-time restriction tied to E-Rate; local screen policies; instructional vs recreational screen use; parents' consent/opt-outs; digital literacy; accessibility; privacy/CIPA.
- Positive edtech claims: demonstrated causal student outcomes; correlation/experience; adaptive personalization; teacher workload; equitable access; assistive tools; cost-effectiveness. Do not combine these under a single generic 'benefits' label.
- E-Rate consultants: individual vs firm registration; definition and exemptions; annual training; Form 5654 and other reporting; percentage vs flat vs contingency fees; vendor-paid intermediaries; conflicts of interest; competitive bidding; applicant administrative burden; consultant value for low-capacity school systems.
- Oversight: concrete alleged misuse (identify who, where, when and how); hypothetical misuse risk; routine program-integrity safeguards; verified enforcement findings. Never convert a general fraud concern into a factual allegation against a named party.
- Legal authority: exact statutory provision and jurisdictional claim; whether a commenter distinguishes program administration from classroom instruction.

Each claim must record whether the source *adopts*, *rejects*, *qualifies*, *quotes*, or *reports someone else's* statement. When the source is ambiguous, label 'unknown / review required'.

## Required validation before a public 'who supports what' result

**Stage A: Coverage and identity.** Inventory every indexed filing, every source document, OCR candidate and model-read window. Check the all-industry shortlist exhaustively, including joint-filer appearances and potential representatives. Preserve unknown professional roles rather than forcing them into 'individual'.

**Stage B: Source-level industry census.** Two reviewers independently inspect *all* filings assigned to edtech vendors, edtech trade associations, E-Rate consulting firms and consultant associations (including joint appearances), coding each policy action and rationale. Resolve any conflict by returning to the original filing.

**Stage C: Balanced comparison sample.** Select an explicitly reproducible stratified sample of school/district, school-professional, school-related-uncertain, library, parent, network/telecom and other/unknown filings, including records where the model found no theme. Do not treat a purposive sample as population-representative. Include extra borderline quote/stance cases and near-duplicate templates.

**Stage D: Error estimates.** Measure category-level precision, recall and confusion for stakeholder classification, thematic arguments, requested actions and speaker attribution against independently adjudicated cases. Report numerator, denominator and uncertainty by stakeholder group; sparse sectors must show count-based results, not visually persuasive percentages alone. Require adjudication before publishing all-corpus position counts. Re-estimate after each material codebook/model revision.

**Stage E: Evidence verification.** For every highlighted rebuttal-worthy claim, recover the complete surrounding source paragraph, check the underlying study/statute/agency record if cited, flag what is actually supported and label unresolved factual claims as unverified. Keep rebuttals distinct from the act of coding comments.

## Publication and user-interface contract

A standard result should show: (1) numerator + correctly scoped denominator; (2) unit (filing, distinct source text, organization or co-signer); (3) whether it is machine candidate, source-checked or double-adjudicated; (4) the full original FCC link; (5) repeated text and co-signer cautions; (6) model-window and OCR coverage; and (7) whether the evidence-context audit hash matches the machine claim ledger.

Support/opposition is not a default machine-coded statistic. A zero automatic match means **no model-detected candidate in the analyzed material**, never 'zero commenters held the view.'

Show separate comparison directions: within-group share of filings raising an argument; group share of all filings raising that argument; and verified position distributions within a precisely defined option. Identical source texts and joint institutional filings remain visible as independent sensitivity columns.

## Current validation status

The research website's machine-derived thematic frequencies are exploratory. The indexed source snapshot contains 3,231 filings; 2,813 had full-text model reads, 417 selected-window reads, one was not modeled, and 205 filings have an OCR candidate. These are dataset-coverage measures, not human verification. The registry has not incorporated an independent adjudicated stakeholder gold standard. Do **not** claim population prevalence, validated stakeholder consensus or confirmed misuse from these datasets.
