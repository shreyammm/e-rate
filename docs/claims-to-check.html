Which claims can we actually verify?

The docket contains assertions about the harms and benefits of screen time, broadband and edtech, consultant incentives, and possible E-rate misuse. Find a claim, inspect exactly what its author wrote, then separate primary filing evidence from independent empirical research.

Publication rule: A disagreement between a school and vendor is evidence of competing positions—not proof either is right. The model can misattribute a quote, reverse the position, or miss relevant context. No automatic claim is marked true or false, and no allegation of misuse is treated as an established violation.

How to check a claim

  • Confirm the author actually endorses the quoted proposition, rather than quoting or criticizing it.
  • Separate descriptive facts, causal allegations, predictions, legal interpretations and value judgments.
  • Find what data or study the commenter relies on; note if none is supplied.
  • Check the technology, age, educational setting, outcome and time period against the cited research.
  • Look for contrary evidence and relevant limitations beyond the original filing.
  • Record a qualified judgment only after independent examination.
